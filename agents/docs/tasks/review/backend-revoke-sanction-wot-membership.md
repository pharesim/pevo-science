# Reclassify revoke as authority sanction; live-threshold WoT membership; ever-sanctioned guard

**Owner:** backend
**Created:** 2026-06-14

The `revoke` authority op currently does double duty: it is broadcast both as a deliberate moderator action AND automatically whenever a WoT member's vouch count drops below threshold (`buildRevocationPayload` carries the fixed reason `"WoT threshold no longer met"`). This conflates a sticky punishment with ordinary loss of standing, and there is no sanction-history check anywhere — a sanctioned account can be silently re-accredited by the WoT auto-path the moment its vouch graph recovers.

This task makes `revoke` mean **sanction only**, makes WoT membership **live-threshold-gated** (self-healing, no revoke broadcast on threshold drop), adds the **ever-sanctioned guard** to the WoT auto-accreditation path, and treats all **legacy revokes as non-sanctions**. Per ARCHITECTURE.md § 2 "Accreditation Lifecycle & Sanctions" (already written), a `revoke` is now a deliberate authority sanction; threshold drops are handled by live membership evaluation with no `revoke` op.

## Why

- A WoT threshold drop is reversible standing, not a punishment. Broadcasting a sticky `revoke` for it (and cascading) wrote a permanent-looking suppression for what is really a transient graph state, and required a fragile cascade with timeout budgets to undo. Live evaluation self-heals with zero broadcasts.
- A true sanction must be sticky: it suppresses membership regardless of vouch support, and the only lift is a deliberate authority `accredit`. Without a `type:"sanction"` discriminator there is no way to distinguish the two, and without an ever-sanctioned check the WoT auto-path re-accredits a sanctioned account behind the moderator's back (the KNOWN GAP in `broadcastWotAccreditation`).
